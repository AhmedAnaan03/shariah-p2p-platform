# PB-35 + PB-36 + PB-29 Repayments, pro-rata payouts and Mudarabah profit reporting

Sprint 4 · stories · 5 + 5 + 5 points · traces to FR11, Objective 4, FR8, IR 2.2.5 · GitHub issues #35, #36, #29

Built as one item: a repayment (PB-35) is only finished once it is paid out to the investors
(PB-36) in the same transaction, and a Mudarabah's period report (PB-29) pays out through the same
split, payout table and reconciler. See `docs/decisions.md`, 26 Nov 2026.

Screenshots for the report are taken once in Sprint 5 (PB-56), so there are none here.

## What was built

### PB-35 Repayments (conventional and Murabahah)

- The borrower's campaign page has a **Repayments** card: wallet balance, the next instalment
  (number, due date, amount, status) and two buttons, **Pay instalment N** and **Pay off in
  full**. Each opens a `ConfirmDialog` with the amount and its breakdown. The payoff dialog shows
  how its amount was worked out.
- `RepaymentService::payNext()` pays the next instalment in one database transaction (READ
  COMMITTED) that locks the campaign, then the schedule rows, then the wallets:
  1. a `repayments` row (amount, capital, return);
  2. one balanced `repayment` transfer from the borrower's wallet into the campaign's escrow,
     refused if the wallet is too low;
  3. the payout to the investors (PB-36);
  4. the row becomes `paid`;
  5. after the last row, the campaign becomes `completed`.
- **In order, one at a time:** the request names the instalment the borrower saw as next. A
  different one is refused ("Instalments are paid in order"), and an already-paid one is refused
  with nothing charged. That is the **double-click guard**, together with the campaign lock, a
  UNIQUE index on `repayments.repayment_schedule_id` and the dialog's spinner.
- **Early payoff** (`payOff()`, worked out by `RepaymentService::quote()`):
  - Conventional: the remaining capital plus the interest of any instalment already due.
    Interest for periods not reached yet isn't charged.
  - Murabahah: the full remaining sale price, because the markup was fixed when the contract
    was agreed.
  - The payoff is refused if its amount changed since it was shown.
  - A voluntary Murabahah rebate (ibra') is a future enhancement.
- **Due / paid / late:** `repayments:mark-late` runs every minute and marks unpaid conventional and
  Murabahah rows `late` after their Maldives due date. It never touches a Mudarabah, and it
  charges no fee. The borrower and the investors see the same state on each row: "Due 26 Jan
  2027", "Due today", "3 days late", "Paid", "Paid late", "Paid early", or "Paid early, interest
  not charged".
- **No late fees or penalty charges for any contract** (decisions.md).

### PB-36 Pro-rata payouts

- Inside the same transaction, the repayment's capital and its return are each split by settled
  pledges with `InstalmentSplit` (largest remainder; a tie goes to the lower investor id). Each
  investor gets one `return_payout` transfer from the escrow, and an `investor_payouts` row
  records the two parts. The escrow is back at zero.
- `LedgerReconciler` gains rules 8 and 9. Every repayment has one transfer of its amount from its
  borrower into its escrow, and its payouts add up exactly to its capital and its return,
  separately. Every payout transfer goes from the right escrow to the right investor, for exactly
  what its row records.
- **Objective 4 test:** four investors with uneven pledges (33.33%, 33.33%, 23.34%, 10%) fund a
  seven-month Murabahah, and every instalment is paid. Each investor's total equals the split
  worked out independently, to the laari, and is within rounding of their exact proportion.
  Everything paid reached the investors, and `wallet:reconcile` passes.
- When the last instalment is paid, the campaign is `completed`. `CampaignCompleted` tells the
  borrower and each investor their own totals.

### PB-29 Mudarabah profit reporting

- For each planned capital return date, the business opens **Report period N** from its
  campaign page. It enters revenue, costs and the capital it is returning, and can attach a
  supporting document (stored encrypted, like every document). A live preview shows what approval
  would do.
- The administrator reviews it under **Business reports**, a list with search, a status filter
  and back links. The review page shows the figures, the document, the capital account and a
  preview of each possible decision. The administrator either:
  - **approves** it; for a loss they also choose its cause, a genuine business loss or
    negligence or misconduct (which needs a reason); or
  - **rejects** it with a reason, and the business can submit a new report for the period.
- **On approval** (`BusinessReportService::approve()`, with the arithmetic in
  `MudarabahSettlement`), in one locked transaction:
  1. **Capital returned** is paid back pro rata, by each investor's outstanding capital.
  2. **A profit first restores earlier genuine losses** (the classical rule), paid pro rata by
     each investor's unrestored loss and recorded separately (`loss_restored_amount`).
  3. **What is left is shared at the agreed ratio.** The investors' share is rounded down to the
     laari and split by capital. The business keeps the rest, which never leaves its wallet.
  4. **A genuine loss** is borne by the investors in proportion to their capital, reducing it
     (never below zero). No money moves; each investor's share is a `loss_amount` row.
  5. **A negligence or misconduct loss** is the business's responsibility. The capital is not
     reduced, the loss is not part of later restoration, and the reason is recorded and
     audit-logged.
  6. **The final settlement:** the last period must leave no capital invested (returned, or
     reduced by a genuine loss). When it is approved, the campaign is `completed` ("Capital
     settled") and everyone is told their totals.
- Each investor's **capital account** is derived from their pledges and payouts, never stored:
  outstanding = pledged − capital returned − loss borne; unrestored loss = loss borne − loss
  restored. Reconciler rule 10 checks that neither is ever below zero, and that a completed
  Mudarabah has nothing outstanding.
- Investors see their own capital account and their part of each approved report. Only the
  business and administrators can open the supporting document.
- Nothing shows a fixed or guaranteed figure. Every new label is in `MudarabahPresenter`,
  `SchedulePresenter::LABELS` or `lib/mudarabah.js` `REPORT_TEXT`, and the wording tests check
  them all for "interest", "guaranteed" and debt words.

### The classical rule and its basis

In classical fiqh, profit protects the capital (al-ribḥ wiqāyah li-ra's al-māl). There is no
profit while the capital is impaired, so a loss is made good from later profit before anything is
shared, and profit and loss are only final when the venture is settled. AAOIFI's Shari'ah Standard
on Mudarabah follows the same principle: the capital provider bears genuine loss, the manager
(mudarib) bears loss caused by his negligence, misconduct or breach of the agreed terms, and
losses are covered by profits before the profit is shared. (Check the exact clause numbers before
citing them in the report.) The platform restores losses each period rather than only at the
final settlement, so investors get their capital back as soon as there is profit to do it. This
is rule-based and **not a formal Shariah certification**.

## Design decisions and alternatives

| Decision | Alternatives | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Borrower → escrow → investors, in one transaction | Borrower straight to each investor; a queued payout job | One balanced transfer to the campaign, and money never waits in escrow; the payout is a separate step the reconciler can check |
| `investor_payouts` table with each part apart | Parts only in ledger descriptions | The reconciler proves the shares add up exactly, by part; the capital account is derived from it |
| Split by investor (summed pledges) | Split by pledge | It is the split the schedule page already showed, so the share shown is the share paid |
| Name the instalment in the request, plus a UNIQUE index | Idempotency keys; disabling the button only | A second click is detected without extra state; the index holds even if the check were bypassed |
| Late is a status set by a scheduled job, no fees | Late fees; computing late only when a page loads | Shariah forbids penalty interest; one rule for all contracts; the database status is what reports read |
| Conventional payoff: capital + interest already due | Full schedule; capital only | No interest for periods not reached; paying off never costs less than an overdue instalment |
| Murabahah payoff: full remaining sale price; ibra' later | Automatic rebate | The sale price is a fixed debt; a compulsory rebate would make it time-based |
| Money moves only on approval | Escrow at submission | The figures are reviewed first; the escrow rule stays unchanged |
| Profit first restores earlier genuine losses | Share every profit at the ratio | The classical rule: no profit while capital is impaired |
| The administrator decides a loss's cause | The business decides | The party who would benefit doesn't judge its own negligence |
| Capital accounts derived, never stored | Running totals on the pledge | Can't drift from the money; reconciler rule 10 proves them |

## Code excerpt (for the sample code section)

`app/Support/MudarabahSettlement.php`: one period of a Mudarabah. A profit first restores earlier
genuine losses, and only the rest is shared at the ratio. A loss is either borne by the investors
in proportion to their capital, or left with the business.

```php
if ($profit > 0) {
    // 2. Restore earlier genuine losses first.
    $restored = min($profit, array_sum($unrestored));
    foreach (self::split($restored, $unrestored) as $id => $cents) {
        $investors[$id]['restored'] = $cents;
    }

    // 3. Share what is left at the agreed ratio.
    $left = $profit - $restored;
    $investorsProfit = intdiv($left * $investorShareBp, 10_000);
    $businessProfit = $left - $investorsProfit;
    foreach (self::split($investorsProfit, $outstanding) as $id => $cents) {
        $investors[$id]['profit'] = $cents;
    }
} elseif ($profit < 0) {
    // 4. A loss: genuine, or the business is liable.
    $loss = -$profit;
    if ($lossCause === null) {
        throw new ReportRefused('Choose whether the loss was a genuine business loss or caused by negligence or misconduct.');
    }
    if ($lossCause === 'business') {
        $remaining = [];
        foreach ($outstanding as $id => $cents) {
            $remaining[$id] = $cents - $investors[$id]['capital'];
        }
        $lossBorne = min($loss, array_sum($remaining));
        foreach (self::split($lossBorne, $remaining) as $id => $cents) {
            $investors[$id]['loss'] = $cents;
        }
    } else {
        $businessLiable = $loss;
    }
}
```

## Accessibility

`npm run check:a11y` on the touched pages, light conventional, 1440/820/390 px: borrower requests,
borrower schedule (repayment card), the Mudarabah business's campaign page and report form, both
wallets, the pledged campaign (an investor's Mudarabah capital account), the admin business
report list and review, and demo controls. The first run found the report table's breakdown
collapsing to zero width, because a `DescriptionList`'s container query doesn't size inside a
table cell. The table now uses `BulletList`, and every page passed with no axe violations.
